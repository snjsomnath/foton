Two observations on the borrowed-light rooms, which hold six of the eight:

- Quadrupling Foton's samples halved their hourly scatter again (29.8 → 15.7, 50.4 → 14.9, 41.0 → 25.4, 60.4 → 17.0, 68.4 → 35.3, 109.8 → 32.1 %), the same response the directly lit rooms showed in the first campaign. A room lit through one internal pane receives each sky patch through very few paths, so per-patch noise is intrinsically high there; a budget that scaled with the fraction of light arriving indirectly would be the next thing to try, but it is a cost that every other room would not need.
- The energy agreement on `interior_glazing` moved from −2.1 / −2.8 % to −0.8 / −0.8 % with the larger budget, so most of what looked like a bias was noise. `three_room_chain`'s third room did not: +5.0 % at MF:1 and +3.6 % at MF:2 on the full coefficients with both engines' halves agreeing, a real excess two panes from the sky. That is the one genuine transport disagreement the corpus has left.

`deep_reflective` is a convergence question, not a disagreement: the reference moves 3.6 % between 8 and 12 bounces, so a 3 % indirect gate cannot be met by any 8-bounce engine in this 0.8-reflectance, 12 m room.

## Files

`suite.json` holds every case's full report (what each `*/mf*/report.json` contained); `suite.md` the status table; per case, `report.md`, `coefficient_comparison.{json,md}`, `converged_annual.json` and, for the acceptance model, `stock_comparison/`. Models are regenerated by `validation/scenes.py` and hashed in each report. Raw stage folders, the coefficient matrices (`radiance_converged_full.npy`, `foton_*.npy`) and the weather cache stay on the machine that ran the campaign (43 GB in all), not in git.
