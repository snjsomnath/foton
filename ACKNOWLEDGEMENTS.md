# Acknowledgements

Foton exists because of decades of open work by other people. We want to name
them. None of the people or groups below wrote Foton or endorse it, and Foton
contains none of their code. The formal credits for papers, data and libraries
are in [NOTICE](NOTICE) and [THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md](THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md).

## Radiance

Greg Ward and the Radiance team at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory built
the lighting simulation that the field still trusts. Every Foton result is
checked against Radiance. Without it we would have had nothing to aim for.

## Daysim and annual daylight metrics

Christoph Reinhart and colleagues developed Daysim and the annual metrics, such
as daylight autonomy and useful daylight illuminance, that Foton computes.

## Accelerad

Nathaniel Jones's Accelerad, from the MIT Sustainable Design Lab, showed that
Radiance-quality daylight simulation can run on the GPU.

## Cyclops

Cyclops, from Foster + Partners, showed that GPU ray tracing for daylight and
environmental analysis can work inside Grasshopper, in everyday design.

## Ladybug Tools

Mostapha Sadeghipour Roudsari, Chris Mackey and the Ladybug Tools community
made daylight simulation part of everyday design in Grasshopper. Foton plugs
into Honeybee rather than replacing it.

## Research we built on

The sky model, sky subdivision and sampling methods come from papers by Richard
Perez, Peter Tregenza, Ilya Sobol', Brent Burley and others. Full citations are
in [NOTICE](NOTICE).

## People who shared their work

People in the building-simulation community who shared their own GPU daylight
work on LinkedIn encouraged us to try this ourselves.

## How Foton was written

Most of Foton's code was written with an AI coding assistant, directed, tested
and checked against Radiance by its authors. That assistant learned from a
great deal of public code. We are grateful to everyone who shares their work in
the open.

## Chalmers

Foton was made for teaching daylight design at Chalmers University of
Technology. Thanks to Alexander Hollberg, supervisor and principal
investigator, for funding this work and for the freedom to explore it.
